## Voz

**A:** In 2023, an Iraqi refugee named Salwan Momika burned copies of the Quran in public squares in Stockholm. Sweden let him. That is what Sweden was — a country where you could do the most offensive thing imaginable, and the state would stand between you and the consequences.

**A:** It didn't.

**A:** On the twenty‑ninth of January, 2025, Momika was live‑streaming from his apartment in Södertälje when men came through the door and shot him dead. On camera. Swedish court documents later named a suspect: a twenty‑four‑year‑old Syrian man, living in Sweden.

**A:** And Sweden had already paid for those burnings once. Turkey spent months blocking Swedish entry into NATO over them, demanding extraditions. A country's entire defence policy, held up over a book.

**A:** This is not a story about one dead man. It is the day Sweden stopped being able to guarantee the one thing it was famous for.

**A:** You can still say anything in Sweden. You just might not survive saying it.

**A:** Should he have been allowed to burn it? Yes or no — comments.

## Notas de precisión

- **Momika era iraquí y cristiano asirio.** No decirlo no es mentir, pero si alguien lo aporta en
  comentarios conviene que la pieza no haya insinuado lo contrario. El guion solo dice "Iraqi refugee".
- El sospechoso está **identificado en documentos judiciales (16-oct-2025)**, no condenado. La voz dice
  *"named a suspect"*. **No decir "su asesino fue un sirio"** hasta que haya condena: es la diferencia
  entre una pieza sólida y una denuncia por difamación.
- *"Turkey spent months blocking… over them"*: Erdoğan citó las quemas **entre** sus razones, junto con
  las extradiciones. La voz dice las dos cosas en la misma frase, que es exacto.
- Momika fue asesinado un día antes de que se leyera el veredicto de su juicio por incitación. No entra
  por tiempo, pero está en la hoja por si Agustín lo quiere.
