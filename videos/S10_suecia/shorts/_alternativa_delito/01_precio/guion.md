## Voz

**A:** In 2022, Sweden had three hundred and ninety shootings. Last year it had a hundred and forty‑seven. Homicides fell to the lowest level since 2012.

**A:** On paper, the crackdown worked.

**A:** Here is what it cost.

**A:** Swedish police were handed powers no Swedish government would have proposed a decade ago. Security zones, where officers stop and search anyone — no suspicion required. Anonymous witnesses in court. Expanded wiretapping. This is the country that spent forty years lecturing the rest of the world about civil liberties.

**A:** And the gangs adapted. As the shootings fell, the bombings climbed. A hundred and thirty‑six detonations in 2024. A hundred and eighty‑nine last year — up thirty‑nine percent. Reported explosives offences are up two hundred and eighty‑nine percent since 2018.

**A:** The guns went quiet. The bombs got louder.

**A:** Sweden did not lose a war. It gave up the country it used to be in order to win one.

**A:** Search anyone, no suspicion needed — worth it? Yes or no, in the comments.

## Notas de precisión

- El mínimo de homicidios **desde 2012** incluye el tiroteo de Örebro (10 muertos, no ligado a bandas),
  que explica casi un cuarto de las muertes por arma de fuego de 2025 (F3). La pieza **no** usa el dato
  de muertes por arma de fuego justamente por eso: usa tiroteos y homicidios totales, que no dependen
  de ese caso.
- "289 % desde 2018" son **delitos denunciados** de explosión (Brå, F6), no detonaciones consumadas.
  Las detonaciones consumadas son el par 136 → 189 (Polisen, F5). La pieza dice las dos cosas con la
  palabra correcta: *detonations* para una, *reported explosives offences* para la otra.
- No se dice ni una vez quién pone las bombas. Ni la policía sueca ni Brå publican esa clasificación.
