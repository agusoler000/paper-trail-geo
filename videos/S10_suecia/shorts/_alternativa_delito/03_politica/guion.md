## Voz

**A:** On Sunday, Sweden voted. The Sweden Democrats — the anti‑immigration party that had grown at every national election in its history — shrank. Down three points. Down eleven seats. They are not even the largest party on the right anymore.

**A:** So the wave broke. Except look at what Sweden did while they were losing it.

**A:** Residency required for citizenship: raised from five years to eight. A language test. A civics test. A self‑sufficiency requirement. No transition period — applications already filed get judged under the new rules. Asylum at its lowest since 1997. A repatriation grant raised by three thousand four hundred percent.

**A:** Every one of those was a Sweden Democrat demand. Every one of them is now Swedish law — passed by other parties, with other parties' names on it.

**A:** Voters did not reject the programme. They kept the programme and dropped the party that wrote it.

**A:** They lost the election because they won the argument.

**A:** So — did they win, or did they lose? One word, in the comments.
